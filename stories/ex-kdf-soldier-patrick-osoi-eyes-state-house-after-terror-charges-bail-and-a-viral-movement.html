Ex-KDF Soldier Patrick Osoi Eyes State House After Terror Charges, Bail and a Viral Movement

News

By Newsroom, 12 Oct, 10:47

Osoi told The Kenya Times he is ready to lead the country and that his first order of business would be to confront corruption. He has also encouraged young Kenyans to go for elective seats and back one another at the ballot. His X account already calls him the 6th President of the Republic of Kenya. No party affiliation or formal candidacy has been reported, so he remains an aspirant for now.

From charcoal seller to the political stage

According to profiles, Osoi was born in Vihiga County in 1990 and grew up in Embu County. He reportedly sold charcoal and books as a young man. He went to Siakago Boys High School and later studied automotive engineering at the National Youth Service School of Engineering. He then earned a degree in Logistics Management from Moi University.

By his own account, he served as a KDF Special Forces soldier and an NIS officer, and later in the US Army. One profile says he was a senior business intelligence analyst and ground forces commander between 2014 and 2020. It adds that he served as an air-assault specialist and armorer in the US Army during the COVID-19 period. Those claims come largely from Osoi and the profiles built on his account, and they haven’t been independently confirmed.

After leaving service, he reportedly founded Capital Choice Investments Ltd, a company said to have interests in real estate, healthcare and media in Kenya and the United States.

The movement that made him famous

Osoi’s public profile rose sharply on July 28, 2025, when he announced the FBI movement. He said he had formed it with ex-officers Jackson Kuria Kimani, popularly known as Cop Shakur, and Kimathi Hiram. The group said it would stand with Kenyans abused by police who follow illegal orders. It named the victims of the June 25, 2024 protests, families of those killed, and officers it said had been abandoned by the system. His post drew more than 260,000 views on X.

Arrest, charges and bail

Police arrested him the next day. His team described it as an abduction by heavily armed men. At Kahawa Law Courts, prosecutors told the court he faced charges including intent to commit a felony, firearm and ammunition possession, and offences linked to terrorism recruitment and facilitation. They said he was planning attacks on police officers around the June 25 and Saba Saba protests.

Osoi and his colleagues rejected the claims. They described the movement as a peaceful civic platform pushing for justice, accountability and constitutionalism, and called for the Terrorism Act to be reviewed. These remain allegations, and they were contested in court.

On August 8, 2025, a magistrate released Osoi and Cop Shakur on Sh50,000 cash bail or a Sh200,000 bond with a surety of the same amount. Their defense was led by Embakasi East MP Babu Owino.
