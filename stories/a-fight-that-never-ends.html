A Fight That Never Ends

News

By Newsroom, 14 Oct, 12:40

Round One: A Man Pushed Out

The fight began as a party quarrel before it became a national one. In February, the ODM National Executive Committee removed Sifuna as Secretary General, and the announcement was made in Mombasa by party leader Oburu Odinga. The party cited discipline and leadership conduct. Sifuna refused to accept it. He said he remained the duly elected Secretary General, vowed to go to court, and claimed his removal followed his firm stance against supporting Ruto’s 2027 reelection.

Whatever the real reason, the ouster changed who Sifuna was to the public. He was no longer a party official defending a line. He had become a man who said he was punished for refusing to back the president, and that gave him a story to tell.

Round Two: The President Responds

Out of the dispute grew Linda Mwananchi. It began with ODM rebels and then drew in leaders from other parties, filling rally grounds in Kisumu, Kisii and across Western Kenya. According to your notes, Ruto responded directly to Sifuna for the first time in July, shortly before a TIFA poll named Sifuna the strongest opposition candidate. That poll put Ruto at 24% and Sifuna at 15%.

From there the attacks went both ways. Ruto claimed that former President Uhuru Kenyatta was funding Linda Mwananchi. Sifuna told him to produce evidence. He said the president had apparently read the claim in a newspaper, and that the movement’s real agenda was to make Ruto a one-term president. No evidence for the funding claim has appeared in the reports I found.

Round Three: The Levy

Sifuna’s next move was on money. He said that if elected he would scrap the affordable housing levy, and he claimed he pays Ksh 24,000 a month in it. At rallies, he called the housing programme a source of white elephants, with cement and steel contracts going to a few people rather than ordinary Kenyans.

On Tuesday in Marakwet East, Ruto answered. He said scrapping the levy would put thousands of jobs at risk and dash the hopes of slum dwellers who want to own homes. He also told residents to register as voters, saying enlistment was low.

Round Four: The Concert

Then came the insult. Sifuna had attended Koffi Olomide’s performance at Fred Obachi Machoka’s 50th media anniversary concert. Ruto mocked the appearance. He said Sifuna and his party had nothing substantive to offer, that all that was left was alcohol, and that asking Kenyans to vote for that insulted their intelligence. In Tiaty, he asked residents to spare him the embarrassment of low voter turnout.

Round Five: The Payslip

Sifuna replied today. He asked whether Ruto was “done ranting” about the 24K, then turned his own payslip into an argument: Ksh 43,000 a month for SHA and Ksh 500,000 in PAYE. He promised that in 2027 they would cut the fat from the budget to bring relief to workers, and he accused the government of spending taxpayers’ money on tea and mandazi at State House.

The pattern is clear. Ruto goes after Sifuna’s character and his housing promise. Sifuna goes after the cost of living and the government’s spending. Neither answers the other’s central point, so each attack leads to another reply.

Why It Keeps Going

The fight continues because each man needs it.

For Ruto, a rival defined as unserious is easier to defeat, and attacking the opposition helps him shift attention from the levies to the opposition’s promises. His call for voter registration suggests he also worries about turnout. For Sifuna, every attack from the president makes him look like the main challenger. He has said repeatedly that Linda Mwananchi will not split the opposition vote, and his meetings with Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka and former Chief Justice David Maraga’s joining the group show he is trying to build a single united ticket.

The harder question for Sifuna is not Ruto. It is whether the opposition will pick him. Rigathi Gachagua says his own supporters want him as candidate, the Luhya Council of Elders has endorsed Sifuna without that becoming a formal nomination, and some in the opposition picture him as a running mate rather than the candidate. Until that is settled, Ruto may be fighting a rival who is not yet the opposition’s choice.

The Fight Ahead

Ten months remain before the vote. The next rounds are easy to predict: more rallies, more claims about taxes and levies, and more replies within a day. The fight will probably end on August 2027, at the ballot box, and not before.
