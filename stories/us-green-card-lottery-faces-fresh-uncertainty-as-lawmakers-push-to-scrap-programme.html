US Green Card Lottery Faces Fresh Uncertainty as Lawmakers Push to Scrap Programme

Business

By Vanywinner KE, 24 Oct, 12:49

Thousands of people hoping to relocate to the United States through the Green Card Lottery are facing fresh uncertainty following a court battle over the delayed 2027 registration period and new efforts by US lawmakers to abolish the programme.

The developments have attracted attention among prospective immigrants in Kenya and across Africa, where the Diversity Visa Lottery offers an opportunity to pursue permanent residence in the United States without necessarily having an employer or close family member sponsor their application.

However, the programme has not been abolished. A federal court has ordered the US government to resume the process, while the administration challenges that decision and some lawmakers seek to end the lottery through legislation.

Court Orders US Government to Reopen Lottery

The latest legal developments follow the failure of the US Department of State to open registration for the 2027 Diversity Visa Programme on its usual schedule.

The department had indicated in November 2025 that changes were being made to the entry process, with registration dates to be announced later. The registration period subsequently remained closed, prompting legal action against the government.

On October 14, 2026, US District Judge Jinsook Ohta issued a preliminary injunction directing the State Department to proceed with the delayed programme. The court later revised the implementation deadlines.

Under the revised order, the department must announce the registration schedule by October 29, 2026, and open registration by November 1. The registration window is required to remain open for 30 days, while selected entrants are to be notified by December 3.

The order also requires the department to process eligible applications within the applicable legal framework and visa allocation.

The ruling followed a lawsuit filed by Red Eagle Law, which challenged the prolonged delay and argued that the government could not effectively suspend a programme established under federal law without adequate legal justification.

The court's intervention does not mean registration is already open. Prospective applicants must wait for the official instructions and confirm the dates published by the State Department.

Trump Administration Appeals Court Decision

The legal battle is not over.

The US government has appealed the ruling and separately sought to suspend the court order while the appeal proceeds.

The appeal raises questions about the government's obligations under the existing immigration law and the court's authority to compel the department to reopen registration after the normal timetable had been disrupted.

If a court grants the government's request to suspend the order, the deadlines could be affected. If the order remains in force, the department will be expected to comply with the revised timetable unless another court decision changes the situation.

As of October 24, 2026, the available reports indicate that the government has filed its appeal and requested a stay. The latest ruling on that request should be confirmed before publication.

The legal uncertainty means prospective applicants should avoid treating the announced deadlines as completely settled until the government publishes its official registration instructions.

Source: Report on the government's appeal.

US Congressman Introduces Bill to End Green Card Lottery

As the court dispute continues, a US lawmaker has introduced legislation seeking to terminate the Diversity Visa Programme.

Representative Keith Self, a Republican from Texas, announced the introduction of House Resolution 10896, commonly referred to as H.R. 10896, on October 22, 2026.

The bill seeks to end the diversity immigrant visa lottery. It was subsequently referred to the House Judiciary Committee for consideration.

In announcing the proposal, Self criticised the court ruling requiring the administration to continue the programme and argued that immigration policy should prioritise US national interests and security.

The proposal adds to the ongoing debate over the future of the lottery, which has faced criticism from some political leaders who favour a different approach to selecting immigrants.

However, the bill remains a proposal. It has not become law, and its introduction does not automatically cancel the ongoing legal process or prevent prospective applicants from participating if registration opens under the existing programme.

For the bill to change federal law, it must proceed through the required legislative process. Its eventual impact will depend on whether it advances through Congress and is enacted.

Source: Representative Keith Self's official announcement.

Other Proposals Also Target the Programme

H.R. 10896 is not the only legislative proposal seeking to change the US immigration system by eliminating the lottery.

Another proposal, the SMARTER Act, was introduced by Representative David Schweikert in September 2026. Its provisions include eliminating the Diversity Visa Programme as part of a broader proposed immigration overhaul.

The proposal seeks to move towards a more skills-focused immigration system and make other changes to family-sponsored immigration.

These efforts reflect a broader political debate over whether the United States should continue using a lottery to allocate some permanent residence opportunities or replace it with a system that places greater emphasis on employment skills and other selection criteria.

Nevertheless, the existence of several proposals does not mean the programme is about to disappear. Each bill must be assessed according to its legislative progress, and none should be described as law unless it has completed the required process.

Why the Green Card Lottery Matters to Kenyans

The Diversity Visa Programme provides an opportunity for eligible people from countries with historically low levels of immigration to the United States to apply for permanent residence.

The programme makes up to 55,000 diversity immigrant visas available annually under its statutory framework, although other legal provisions can reduce the number available in a particular year.

Unlike some employment-based immigration routes, the lottery does not generally require an applicant to obtain a job offer from a US employer before entering.

Applicants must, however, meet the programme's education or work-experience requirements, satisfy the applicable country and other eligibility rules, and complete the required immigration procedures.

For Kenyans hoping to relocate, the current developments raise two separate questions: whether the delayed 2027 registration period will open as ordered by the court, and whether Congress will eventually change the law governing the programme.

The answers will depend on the outcome of the legal proceedings, the official registration announcement and the progress of any legislation introduced in Congress.

Will the Lottery Be Cancelled?

The possibility of future abolition cannot be ruled out, because Congress can amend the law governing the programme. However, a proposal to end the lottery is not the same as a final decision to abolish it.

For now, the court's order and the proposed legislation are separate matters. The court dispute concerns the administration of the programme under the existing legal framework, while lawmakers are considering whether that framework should be changed.

Anyone claiming that the Green Card Lottery has already been permanently abolished should therefore provide evidence of an enacted law or another legally effective decision.

Similarly, applicants should not assume that the court ruling guarantees that registration will proceed without further delays, given the pending appeal.

Warning to Applicants: Avoid Green Card Scams

The uncertainty surrounding the programme may create opportunities for fraudsters to target people eager to move to the United States.

Prospective applicants should be cautious of individuals claiming to have inside information, offering guaranteed selection or demanding payment in exchange for a promise of winning.

The US Department of State advises applicants to use its official Diversity Visa website and to be careful when dealing with intermediaries. Selection in the lottery is not a guarantee of a visa or permanent residence.

The official entry website is https://dvprogram.state.gov/. Applicants should use it only when registration is officially open and follow the instructions issued for the relevant programme year.

Readers should also consult the US Department of State's official immigration guidance at https://travel.state.gov/ for updates on eligibility, application requirements and registration dates.

What Happens Next?

The immediate developments to watch are the outcome of the government's request to suspend the court order, the official announcement of registration dates and any further action on legislation seeking to abolish the programme.

For Kenyan applicants, the most important message is that the Green Card Lottery has not been permanently abolished by the introduction of H.R. 10896. At the same time, the court's order does not remove the possibility of further legal delays.

Until the situation becomes clearer, prospective applicants should prepare their documents, follow official announcements and avoid making financial commitments based on unverified claims.

The coming weeks could be significant for people hoping to enter the 2027 lottery, as developments in both the courts and Congress may shape the programme's immediate future.
