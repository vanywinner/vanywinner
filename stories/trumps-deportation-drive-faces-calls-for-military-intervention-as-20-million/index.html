Trump’s Deportation Drive Faces Calls for Military Intervention as 20 Million Target Looms

News

By Newsroom, 8 Nov, 12:44

The push to remove millions of undocumented immigrants from the United States is facing renewed calls for a more aggressive enforcement strategy, with proposals to involve the military in a nationwide deportation campaign. Supporters of the approach argue that existing immigration agencies cannot manage an operation of such magnitude within the proposed two-year timeline.

The proposal centres on the claim that approximately 20 million people are living in the United States illegally, a figure that proponents of mass deportation want reduced to zero. They argue that the scale of the challenge demands a national security response rather than the conventional administrative process handled by immigration authorities.

Under the suggested plan, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) would work alongside military personnel, who would be granted specific responsibilities in identifying, detaining and facilitating the removal of individuals found to be unlawfully residing in the country. Advocates believe that expanding the enforcement workforce would accelerate deportations and ease pressure on immigration agencies.

The argument has also been framed around the financial and social implications of undocumented immigration. Supporters contend that public resources, including housing, healthcare and education, are under increasing pressure, while concerns about crime and border security continue to shape the national debate.

However, the proposal raises significant questions about the legal authority of the military in domestic immigration enforcement, the cost of large-scale detention and the protection of individual rights. Immigration experts have also questioned the 20 million estimate, with various research organisations producing lower figures for the undocumented population.

The plan reflects a broader political divide over immigration policy in the United States. While supporters view mass deportation as necessary to restore border control and enforce existing laws, critics warn that a military-led approach could create serious legal, humanitarian and economic consequences.

Whether such an operation could be carried out within two years remains uncertain. What is clear is that the debate over immigration enforcement is increasingly focused not only on the number of people to be removed, but also on the extraordinary measures the government may be willing to take to achieve that goal.
