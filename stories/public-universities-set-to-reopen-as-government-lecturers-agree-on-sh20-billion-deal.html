Public Universities Set to Reopen as Government, Lecturers Agree on Sh20 Billion Deal

News

By Vanywinner KE, 28 Oct, 22:06

Learning in Kenya’s public universities is expected to resume next week after the government and striking university lecturers reached a Sh20 billion agreement aimed at ending a dispute that has disrupted academic activities for nearly four weeks.

Universities Academic Staff Union (UASU) Secretary-General Constantine Wasonga confirmed the verbal agreement on Wednesday evening, October 28, saying both sides had resolved the financial issues that triggered the strike.

“We have a deal on the financials,” Wasonga said.

The agreement is expected to be formally signed on Friday, October 30, following the documentation of its terms by technical teams representing the government and the unions. Lecturers are expected to return to lecture halls on Monday, November 2, paving the way for the resumption of learning in affected institutions.

According to a ministry official, the Sh20 billion will be disbursed in three phases, although details of the payment schedule have yet to be made public.

The industrial action began on October 2 after UASU, the Kenya Universities Staff Union (KUSU) and the Kenya Union of Domestic, Hotels, Educational Institutions, Hospitals and Allied Workers (KUDHEIHA) protested delays in implementing the 2025–2029 Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA).

The strike paralysed learning across 35 public universities, leaving students uncertain about the resumption of lectures and the adjustment of academic calendars.

Negotiations between the government and the unions faced repeated setbacks, with the unions rejecting an earlier Sh12 billion offer on October 12. The latest Sh20 billion agreement marks a significant step towards resolving the dispute and restoring normal academic activities.

Pressure to end the strike intensified on Tuesday when police lobbed tear gas at University of Nairobi students who had blocked Waiyaki Way while demanding an immediate resolution to the lecturers’ standoff.

Despite the breakthrough in negotiations, the strike remains technically in force until the agreement is formally signed and the unions officially call off the industrial action.

Students and university administrations will therefore be watching Friday’s signing closely, as it is expected to determine whether the planned November 2 reopening proceeds as anticipated.
