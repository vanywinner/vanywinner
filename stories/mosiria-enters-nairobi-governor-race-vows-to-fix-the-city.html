Mosiria Enters Nairobi Governor Race, Vows to “Fix” the City

News

By Newsroom, 13 Oct, 13:19

On Sunday, October 11, Geoffrey Mosiria stood in a church hosted by Bishop Margaret Wanjiru and declared that he wants to be Nairobi’s next governor. It was an awkward announcement, because he currently works for the man he wants to unseat. Mosiria is the county’s Chief Officer for Citizen Engagement and Customer Service, and Governor Johnson Sakaja is his boss.He had floated the idea ten days earlier, on October 1, saying former governor Mike Sonko had advised him to aim for the top seat. He has also said he is seeking a meeting with Jubilee leader Fred Matiang’i in the hope of an endorsement. His pitch is “Mr Fix-it”: one term, he says, is enough to fix Nairobi. He also says Sakaja tried to dim his rising star. His path to this point has been a busy one, moving from health to environment in August 2024 and then to citizen engagement.

The campaign trail

The Wanjiru connection may complicate things. Mosiria floated the bishop as a possible running mate, but Matiang’i has since backed her for governor, and she says she will not withdraw.

A record built on crackdowns

Mosiria’s public profile rests on enforcement. In 2025 he announced rules switching off matatu music in the CBD, along with night-time noise measurements that led to equipment being seized the next morning. He went after churches in Pipeline and clubs in Kilimani, and some clubs went to court to block enforcement.

He has also led inspections of waste disposal by CBD hotels and businesses, and more recently urged building owners to repaint and light up their properties.

His record on street people is mixed. In June 2025 he said he took a woman with two children home to Kakamega, with well-wishers helping with shopping. Around the same time, he called for a crackdown on people who use children to exploit public sympathy. Critics may point to that contrast.

His methods have drawn pushback. Mpasho headlines mention an outcry in August 2026 over jaywalkers being made to clean streets without gloves, and a KSh10 million lawsuit by Oga Obinna, though I only have the headlines, not the details.

The Babu Owino feud

The campaign is also colliding with an old rivalry. In a September post, Mosiria said his dispute with Embakasi East MP Babu Owino goes back to student politics at the University of Nairobi (SONU). He claims goons sent by Babu attacked him at the Kikuyu and Upper Kabete campuses and demanded money, adding that he has forgiven but not forgotten. Earlier this year, he called Babu an extortionist and said he would expose him. I found no response from Babu to the campus allegations.

The two have clashed over current politics too. On September 25, Mosiria disputed Babu’s claim that his party registered 660,008 members in six days, saying the real figure was about 46,000. Babu, who has declared himself the frontrunner for governor, has criticised the hawker evictions as harassment of the urban poor. Mosiria replied that Babu was weaponising poverty.

The attacks have gone both ways. An old video resurfaced of Babu making claims about why Mosiria left the University of Nairobi, and Mosiria’s lawyer wrote to the university seeking clarification. I found no confirmation either way.

What to watch

Mosiria’s candidacy raises several questions. Can a serving official run against his own boss? Will Matiang’i’s backing of Wanjiru close the door on his hoped-for endorsement? And will a record of high-profile crackdowns draw voters or put them off? With the election still about a year away, he and Babu Owino look set to keep trading blows.
